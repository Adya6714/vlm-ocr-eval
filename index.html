Abstract

A diagnostic instrument for Indic document OCR

This repository asks a narrow question: when a document VLM misreads Devanagari or Bengali, why — and can we tell the difference between a model that cannot see the glyph and a model that is confidently guessing from its language prior?

Answering that requires a model you own end to end. So this project builds a small instrument from scratch (~19.5M params, zero Indic pretraining), with a controlled glyph-frequency renderer and a Stage 0 equivalence taxonomy — then probes confidence on blank and real images.

Measured in-checkout: ~20.4% of Tesseract non-exact preds are Tier 1 encoding noise; glyph-frequency TV ≤ 0.08. Reported (Colab; jsonl not in tree): Probe 3 confidence barely drops on blank pages; Probe 5 top-bucket accuracy ≈0.10. Stages 2b, 3, 5, 6, and Probe 5b remain deliberately out of scope.

Research apparatus — not a product

Confidence isn't reading.
Here's how to tell the difference.

A small OCR model, built from scratch with zero Indic pretraining, exists for one reason: to measure whether a model's confidence comes from actually seeing the glyph, or from guessing what a script "usually" contains. This is the instrument, and what it found.

The core idea
2. Method

Three things this isn't: CER, a benchmark, a leaderboard entry

Three deliberate departures from "run OCR, compute error rate" — each one is a methodological choice, not a shortcut.

Not every diff is an error

Devanagari has many same-sound, different-byte encodings — ZWJ/ZWNJ joiners, khanda-ta, anusvara vs. conjunct nasal, danda vs. period. A two-tier equivalence check (encoding-level, then phonetic-level via ISO 15919) separates real misreads from formatting noise. On real Tesseract output, about one in five apparent errors was Tier 1 encoding noise, not a misread.

Exposure is a dial, not an observation

Rather than correlating accuracy against whatever frequencies happen to occur, the renderer sets glyph-cluster frequency directly — natural, flattened, inverted — matched on data volume, verified within TV ≤ 0.08 of target. Nine training runs (3 conditions × 3 seeds) turn a correlation into something closer to a causal claim.

Confidence is what's being measured

A model that fails loudly is manageable. One that fails fluently on a low-resource script is dangerous. The blank/noise control (above) and the calibration probe exist to catch exactly that — confidence that doesn't track correctness.

Say this

The one-liner: this isn't another CER leaderboard. I separate encoding noise from real misreads, I set glyph exposure as a causal dial, and I treat confidence as the thing worth measuring — because fluent overconfidence on a low-resource script is the failure mode that actually ships.

Why it existsStage 0's error taxonomy needs ground-truth/prediction pairs per engine; this script produces the prediction half without judging correctness.

src/eval/error_taxonomy.py

What it doesClassifies every engine prediction as an exact match, encoding variant, phonetic variant, a genuine-error label, or still unreviewed — then reports the fractions per engine.

Why it existsThis is Stage 0's deliverable: the interview claim rests on how many apparent OCR errors are really just encoding or phonetic variants.

src/eval/equivalence_tables.py

What it doesA hand-curated lookup of same-sound, different-bytes Unicode pairs so the scorer can mark those diffs as not errors.

Why it existsTier 1 is an uncontroversial encoding fact, kept separate from phonetic judgment calls (DECISIONS.md #4, #8).

What it doesChecks whether two differently spelled strings are the same word by transliterating both to ISO 15919 Latin and comparing.

Why it existsTier 2 is a judgment call about sameness, reported separately from Tier 1 so arguable matches never inflate the encoding-noise number (DECISIONS.md #8).

What it doesInteractive side-by-side viewer that walks ground truth against each engine's prediction and records human labels for unexplained diffs.

What it doesResamples or synthesizes source text so grapheme-cluster frequencies match natural, flattened, or inverted targets within a fixed tolerance.

What it doesBuilds a reusable bank of real document layouts (Internet Archive, India.gov PDFs, Wikipedia Indic pages) instead of hand-drawn frames.

The renderer isn't a data utility — it's the experimental apparatus. Glyph frequency is a dial I set, verified within TV ≤ 0.08 of target, so Probe 1 can make something closer to a causal claim about exposure.

Layout bank and degradation profile are still partial: extractors exist, but the real-document pools aren't finished, and I say that out loud.

Findings so far
4. Results

Early, single-condition, reported as such

BOOK.md distinguishes measured (reproduced in this checkout) from reported (Colab write-up; probe jsonl not checked in). Both kinds appear below — labeled.

Measured · Stage 0

Encoding noise is real

Of Tesseract predictions that were not exact match, ~20.4% were Tier 1 encoding variants (n=180). Surya: ~16.9% of non-exact. Large UNREVIEWED remains — report is real, not final.

Measured · Stage 1

Exposure dial hits the gate

Hindi GT slice TV: natural 0.000, flattened 0.047, inverted 0.005 (all ≤ 0.08). Instrument architecture: ~19.5M params. Tier A verify mean render ~82 ms.

Reported · Probes 3 / 5

Confidence not grounded yet

One hindi/natural/seed0 checkpoint, 30 samples. Numbers below are from that Colab run — reproduce via BOOK Appendix E once checkpoints are local.

Probe 3 — reading vs. guessing (reported)

Mean confidence: 0.9929 on real line crops, 0.9898 on blank images, 0.9877 on matched noise. The gap is nearly nothing — this checkpoint's confidence barely distinguishes real text from an empty page.

Probe 5 — calibration (reported)

Every one of 30 sampled predictions landed in the top confidence bucket (0.90–1.00). Actual accuracy in that bucket: 0.10. When this checkpoint says it's nearly certain, it's right about one time in ten.

Read together: at this training scale, the instrument isn't grounding its confidence in the image or in correctness. That's an expected, legible failure mode for a small model at 5000 steps on a thin corpus — not a contradiction of the design, but exactly the kind of thing the probe suite exists to surface before it reaches production. Three independent lines already converge without deferred stages: encoding variants are first-class noise, exposure can be set as a dial, and owning decode-time confidence is what makes blank-control askable.

Say this

This is the part I'd lead with — the model's confidence barely dropped when I showed it a blank image. That's not a bug, that's the whole reason this project exists: to catch exactly that kind of silent overconfidence before it ships.

And I'd immediately add the honesty caveat: one checkpoint, thirty samples, early training. The signal is coherent; it is not a final claim.

6. Future work

What isn't built, and why not

Named clearly rather than left implicit. Each of these is a real design, not an afterthought — just gated on more time than a compressed build allows.

Stage 2bthe demo model

LoRA on a pretrained VLM

Multi-module training (base model + separate layout + reading-order modules), SFT then RLVR — a different project shape, not gated on the instrument's findings. Its one built prerequisite: benchmark_base_models.py, measuring T4 memory fit for SmolDocling-256M vs. LightOnOCR-1B before committing to either.

Stage 3structure metrics

Reading order + table binding

Kendall-tau reading-order metric and header-cell binding accuracy, both blocked on Stage 1's real layout bank (form / table-embedded categories still partial).

Stage 5Sarvam transfer

Does the finding rhyme at production scale

Pre-specified rank-correlation test, permutation null, ~200-page cached budget, run on clean and degraded tiers. A null result would still be reported.

Stage 6triage cascade

Router quality, not cost savings

Sweep escalation thresholds offline against the Stage 5 cache, against three baselines. Framed as router quality on purpose — the instrument isn't expected to beat Tesseract on raw accuracy.

Probe 5bzero-shot floor

Santhali / Kashmiri, true zero exposure

Inference-only, no training needed — the renderer can't produce these scripts (no font support), but real Santhali/Kashmiri images already exist in the corpus and could run this cheaply.

RLVRcoverage ablation

Reward gaming by saying less

Planned reward: accuracy + TEDS + reading-order correlation − a coverage term. Without coverage, accuracy rises by omitting text. The only planned ablation removes that term and quantifies silence — described in BOOK Ch. 6, not built this phase.

Say this

I'd name what's missing before they ask. Demo model, structure metrics, Sarvam transfer, triage cascade — each is a real design with a reason it didn't ship in this phase, not a vague backlog item.

Constraints, stated plainly
5. Limitations

What more compute would buy

Everything here ran on a free Colab T4. That bounded real decisions — worth being explicit about what it bounds.

  • Longer training — the overconfidence above is consistent with undertraining. 10–50× the steps would separate "hasn't learned to use the image yet" from "systematically fails to ground confidence in vision" — currently indistinguishable from the data.
  • A larger corpus — 100 rendered pages against a 60-line source means heavy reuse. More source text sharpens the glyph-frequency manipulation and reduces memorization risk.
  • Full probe sampling — probes currently sample 30 items for time. The full manifest would tighten every number and make per-glyph-class breakdowns statistically meaningful.
  • All four languages — Santhali and Kashmiri need font support and a separate render path; Bengali is a full nine-run sweep of its own.
  • Stage 2b at all — LoRA on a 1B model plus two resident auxiliary modules is the specific thing a single T4 makes painful.
Say this

Everything ran on a free Colab T4. That bound fp16, model size, step count, and sample size. More compute wouldn't change the design — it would separate undertraining from a systematic failure to ground confidence in vision, which I can't distinguish yet.
